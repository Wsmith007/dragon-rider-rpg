
# World Overview

The world is divided among four major races:
- Humans
- Elves
- Dwarves
- Veyrin

All four races existed long before dragon bonding was discovered.

Dragons originally existed as wild apex creatures and were not bonded to any race.

Magic existed prior to dragon bonding, but magical ability was not equally distributed among races or individuals.

Dragon bonding and magic are connected, but they are not the same thing.

---

# Lifespans

## Dragons

Dragons do not naturally die from aging.

However, they may still die from:
- combat
- disease
- magical corruption
- catastrophic injury

Dragons are ancient, intelligent beings whose lifespans often stretch across entire eras of civilization.

---

## Elves

Elves do not naturally die from aging.

Like dragons, they may still die from external causes.

Elves reproduce very rarely, and births are considered culturally significant events.

Almost every elf is born with the ability to use magic.

Elven culture tends to value patience, long-term balance, and preservation of knowledge.

---

## Dwarves

Dwarves may live for several centuries, with exceptional individuals reaching approximately 700 years of age.

Dwarven civilization values memory, structure, craftsmanship, and stability.

Their long lifespans allow them to pursue projects and traditions across generations.

---

## Veyrin

Veyrin lifespans vary significantly due to magical instability and environmental adaptation.

Most live far shorter lives than dwarves, though exceptional individuals may reach approximately 200 years of age.

Veyrin society is shaped heavily by survival, adaptation, and distrust of centralized power.

---

## Humans

Humans have the shortest average lifespan among the major races.

Exceptional humans may reach approximately 100 years of age.

Human civilizations often emphasize rapid adaptation, ambition, expansion, and innovation due to their comparatively brief lives.

---

# Magic

Magic is an innate ability possessed by some individuals across all races.

Not every individual is born capable of using magic.

A person may:
- use magic without bonding to a dragon
- bond to a dragon without originally possessing magic
- possess both abilities simultaneously

Magic ability is influenced by bloodlines, natural affinity, and unknown spiritual or environmental factors.

---

# Elven Magical Affinity

Elves possess the strongest natural magical affinity among the races.

It is extremely rare for an elf child to be born without magical capability.

Because elves reproduce infrequently, each birth is considered culturally important.

Elven magical traditions are deeply connected to language, resonance, memory, and emotional discipline.

---

# Dragons Before Bonding

Before dragon bonding, dragons were feared as ancient apex beings.

They:
- could not be controlled
- rarely interacted peacefully with civilization
- were viewed as natural disasters, omens, or divine entities

Some ancient cultures worshipped dragons while others feared or hunted them.

At this stage, dragons did not willingly hatch alongside the races.

---

# Discovery of Dragon Bonding

The elves were the first race to establish stable dragon bonds.

Using advanced true-name magic and psychic resonance techniques, they discovered that dragons could form conscious bonds with compatible individuals.

This process allowed:
- stable dragon hatching
- emotional synchronization
- long-term rider partnerships

The first bonded dragons dramatically shifted the balance of power in the world.

Dragon riders quickly became symbols of authority, influence, and military power.

---

# Dragon Bonding and Magic

Dragon bonding does not require prior magical ability.

However, bonding with a dragon may awaken magical capability within individuals who were not originally born with access to magic.

Over time, the number of magic users within every race increased due to the influence of dragon bonding.

This effect was especially significant among Humans, Dwarves, and Veyrin populations.

As dragon bonding spread, magic itself became more politically important and more widely feared.

---

# Rise of the Rider Powers

After observing the elves, Humans eventually adapted dragon bonding methods for themselves.

Human riders expanded rapidly and became politically and militarily influential.

As rider power grew, the Dwarves and Veyrin were eventually forced to develop their own bonding methods to avoid domination by Elf and Human rider factions.

Each race ultimately developed a different philosophy toward dragon bonding.

---

# Bonding Philosophies

## Elven Philosophy

Dragon bonding is sacred and should remain balanced, disciplined, and spiritually guided.

Elves generally believe dragons should never become tools of conquest or unchecked expansion.

---

## Human Philosophy

Dragon bonding should be expanded, adapted, and made accessible.

Humans often view dragon riders as symbols of progress, power, and opportunity.

---

## Dwarven Philosophy

Dragon bonding must be stabilized, regulated, and reinforced through structure and engineering.

Dwarves tend to distrust uncontrolled magical systems and value predictability over instinct.

---

## Veyrin Philosophy

Dragon bonding is dangerous, unstable, and inseparable from survival.

Many Veyrin distrust centralized authority and reject the idea that dragons should belong to governments or ruling orders.

---

# Current Political Tension

The game takes place during a period of escalating tension between the races.

Conflicts are emerging over:
- dragon territory
- rider influence
- magical regulation
- border expansion
- control of ancient dragon sites
- access to dragon eggs
- authority over bonded riders

Many fear another catastrophic conflict between rider factions.

Some groups seek domination while others seek coexistence.

Ancient alliances are weakening as each race becomes increasingly protective of its own riders and interests.

---

# Core Conflict

The central question of the world is:

Should dragons belong to nations, riders, or themselves?

The races disagree fundamentally on:
- who should control dragon bonding
- how dragons should be treated
- whether rider power stabilizes or threatens civilization
- whether dragons should remain independent beings or political assets

---

# Main Themes

- unity vs nationalism
- balance vs control
- coexistence with dragons
- emotional bonds vs engineered systems
- political misuse of power
- fear of imbalance
- trust between fundamentally different cultures
- power gained through connection

---

# Possible Endgame Direction

The player may eventually attempt to create a unified rider order capable of preserving peace between all races.

However, different factions may instead seek:
- domination
- separation
- dragon liberation
- strict regulation
- military supremacy
- destruction of rival rider traditions

The future of dragon bonding — and the balance of power between the races — may ultimately depend on whether cooperation is possible at all.
