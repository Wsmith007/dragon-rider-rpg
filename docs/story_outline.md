# Narrative Overview

The story takes place during a period of escalating political tension between Humans, Elves, Dwarves, and Veyrin rider factions.

Dragon riders have become symbols of military power, political influence, and cultural identity.

Ancient alliances between the races are weakening as each civilization increasingly fears domination by the others.

The player begins the game already bonded to a young dragon.

The rider-dragon relationship becomes the emotional and narrative core of the story.

As tensions rise, the player becomes increasingly involved in the growing instability surrounding dragons, magic, and the future of rider society.

---

# Player Role

The player is a newly bonded dragon rider.

The player selects one of four races:
- Human
- Elf
- Dwarf
- Veyrin

Each race experiences:
- different political relationships
- unique rider traditions
- different bond philosophies
- distinct gameplay styles
- different social expectations

The player and dragon begin inexperienced and emotionally unrefined.

Both grow together throughout the story.

---

# Core Narrative Themes

- unity vs division
- balance vs control
- fear of power
- emotional trust
- coexistence with dragons
- political misuse of strength
- cultural misunderstanding
- independence vs authority

The story focuses heavily on relationships:
- rider and dragon
- race and race
- civilization and dragons
- control and cooperation

---

# Narrative Structure

The story is divided into four major acts.

Each act escalates:
- political instability
- dragon conflict
- rider influence
- synchronization complexity
- emotional strain

The dragon bond evolves alongside the story progression.

---

# Act 1 — The Early Bond

The player begins already bonded to a young dragon.

The bond is unstable, emotionally intense, and poorly understood by both rider and dragon.

Early story focuses on:
- learning synchronization
- basic combat survival
- communication development
- introduction to rider politics
- racial tensions
- local conflicts

The player quickly learns that dragons are viewed very differently by each race and faction.

The act ends with the player becoming publicly recognized as a rider.

This draws political attention and begins larger conflicts.

---

# Act 2 — Rising Tensions

The player travels between territories controlled by different races and rider factions.

Political instability grows as:
- territorial disputes increase
- dragon incidents escalate
- factions compete for influence
- ancient rider agreements weaken

The player encounters:
- competing rider philosophies
- extremist factions
- anti-rider movements
- dragon independence advocates

Dragon communication deepens significantly during this act.

The player begins understanding dragons as fully intelligent beings rather than companions or weapons.

The act ends with a major political or military breakdown between rider factions.

---

# Act 3 — Fracture

Open conflict begins spreading between races and rider powers.

The player becomes increasingly influential due to:
- dragon bond strength
- synchronization ability
- political reputation
- relationships with multiple factions

The player learns deeper truths regarding:
- early dragon bonding history
- how bonding changed civilization
- the long-term consequences of rider militarization
- growing instability between dragons and civilization

Bond strain intensifies during this act.

Synchronization and instability become increasingly dangerous and emotionally demanding.

Some dragons and riders begin rejecting traditional political structures entirely.

---

# Act 4 — The Future of Riders

The world approaches large-scale collapse or transformation.

The player must decide:
- whether rider society can be unified
- whether dragons should remain tied to governments
- whether the races can coexist peacefully
- whether dragon bonding itself should change

The player's relationships, political choices, and bond development shape the final outcome.

The dragon becomes a fully realized narrative partner during this stage.

Communication may become nearly seamless between rider and dragon.

---

# Dragon Narrative Role

Dragons are not tools or mounts.

They are intelligent beings with:
- personalities
- desires
- fears
- instincts
- political significance

Different dragons may hold different views regarding:
- riders
- civilization
- war
- independence
- coexistence

As bonds deepen, dragons become increasingly active participants in the story rather than supporting companions.

---

# Race Perspective Differences

Each race experiences the world differently.

## Humans
Experience political expansion, rapid rider growth, and fears of instability.

## Elves
Experience cultural responsibility, preservation concerns, and fear of rider corruption.

## Dwarves
Experience concerns over control, stability, and the dangers of uncontrolled magic.

## Veyrin
Experience marginalization, survival pressure, and distrust of centralized authority.

These perspectives alter:
- dialogue
- faction relationships
- political opportunities
- narrative interpretation
- rider expectations

---

# Major Narrative Questions

The story revolves around several central questions:

- Should dragons belong to nations or themselves?
- Can riders exist without becoming political weapons?
- Is instability caused by dragons, civilizations, or both?
- Can fundamentally different races coexist peacefully?
- Is emotional synchronization strength or danger?
- Should dragon bonding continue evolving?

The game intentionally avoids simple good-versus-evil faction structures.

---

# Possible Endings

Possible outcomes may include:

## Unified Rider Order
A cooperative rider structure forms between the races.

---

## Dominant Faction Ending
One race or ideology gains control over rider society.

---

## Dragon Liberation
Dragons reject political control and separate from civilization.

---

## Fragmented World
The races isolate themselves and rider society collapses.

---

## Overbond Catastrophe
Extreme synchronization or instability fundamentally alters rider-dragon relationships.

---

# Vertical Slice Story Scope

The first playable prototype focuses only on:
- one small region
- early rider recognition
- initial dragon communication
- basic political tension
- beginner synchronization mechanics
- small-scale combat encounters

The first prototype is intended to establish:
- emotional dragon bonding
- cooperative combat
- evolving communication
- race identity
- rider tension

Large-scale political and world systems are intentionally postponed until the core rider-dragon relationship feels successful.
